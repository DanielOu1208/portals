# Devices: host tabs that fit, a Mac app's own name, portals renamed across the net

2026-10-28, three asks on branch `n15/devices` (from main 0f43a5c1).

## 1. Host tabs in the sidebar (Portals/Sidebar/MacTabs.swift)

"on the sidebar when theres many devices just show the icon for the non selected device so
more devices can be shown without stuff getting cut off". With four hosts on a 306 to 360
point sidebar each tab was about 84 points and every name was cut ("Mac…", "Danie…").

**When:** whenever the names would not all fit, measured, not a set number of hosts. The row
stays as it is (every tab named, all as wide as each other) while every name fits in its
share; once one would be cut, the tab of the host shown keeps its icon and name and takes
the rest of the row, and the others are their icon alone. Why measured: two or three hosts
with short names keep all their names, which say more than icons do (every laptop has the
same icon), and four short names on a Mac's wider sidebar fit too; a fixed number would cut
three long names or hide four short ones. The decision is the same whichever host is shown:
each name is measured as the shown tab sets it (semibold), and the room the add-folder
button takes is kept while any host offers folders, so swiping across the spaces never
flips the row between the two looks.

**How:** a `ViewThatFits` over the two rows. The named row is laid out by a small `Layout`
whose ideal width is its widest tab times the count, so it is chosen only when every name
fits its equal share; the compact row is the fallback, and scrolls when even it does not fit
(the shown tab no narrower than 84 points). An icon-only tab (34 points on a Mac, 40 for a
finger) keeps the connection's dot, at the icon's corner, its accessibility label (the
host's name) and value (its status), its pointer tip ("Mac mini: Connected"), and its menu,
whose title now names the host (a long press on an iPhone or iPad). A stopped host's icon tab
shows its orange dot; its Retry is in the menu, and on its tab once shown. Dragging a tab
along the row works with tabs of two widths: a tab lands past each whose middle its edge
crossed, and the others slide by its own width.

## 2. A Mac app's name in the net (Portals/App/Net.swift)

`Net.device` used `UIDevice.current.name`, which since iOS 16 is the model alone ("iPhone",
"iPad") and on Mac Catalyst "iPad". The name a device joins with is now:

1. The name it was given in a net (item 3), kept in its membership (`NetMembership.givenName`),
   through leaves and switches, as a Mac keeps a name set from the app.
2. On a Mac: the name in the net of the bridge on this same Mac. When pairing with a
   bridge's own link, that bridge's machine (`HostMachine`, `Platform.isThisMac`) and the
   name its join code's link carries (the net's name for it); else a host of the fleet whose
   machine is this Mac (`HostModel.isOnThisMac`), by its net name or its own. Else "Mac".
   Never "iPad".
3. On an iPhone or iPad, `UIDevice.current.name` as before.

## 3. Portals renamed across the net

Feature **`net_rename_devices`** (bridge). `net.rename {member, name, after?, net?}` takes a
device's id as well as a Mac's, from the same callers (a device, the password, the command);
the wire shape is unchanged. A device's entry carries `named` and `named_by` once renamed,
stamped and merged exactly as a Mac's name is (`max(now, named + 1, after + 1)`, the later
stamp wins whichever copy of the entry does; the entry's `changed` and `by` stay so a rename
never undoes a removal). Refusals: "There is no member X in this net." for an id the list
does not have or has removed; a name check in the member's word ("A device's name cannot be
empty."). A device that joins again (re-added, or switched back) replaces its entry as
before, and its name is stamped as a rename made then, at the entry's own `changed` (past
the one it had), so the name it joins with is the net's on every Mac rather than an older
given one winning the merge elsewhere, also one given on a Mac the joining one had not
heard from (Codex review). A device's rename is stamped past its entry's `changed`, so the
app keeps as `givenName` only a name it was given (stamped past `changed`, or at it by
another Mac than `by`), not one it joined with (Codex's re-checks).

Bridge: `members.ts` reads `named`/`named_by` on a device's entry too; `net.ts` `rename`
takes devices, `joinDevice` stamps past a renamed entry, a joining Mac takes along later
names of devices as of Macs; `features.ts`, `methods.ts` (summary), `portals.ts` and the
guide (`portals net rename [MEMBER] NAME`), `contract.json`.

An older bridge drops a device's stamp, keeps the name it had for an entry it holds, and
takes the renamed one for an entry it does not; its copies undo no rename on the new ones.
An older app drops the stamp and keeps the name it held.

App: `NetWire` reads the stamps on a device's entry; `BridgeWire.Feature.netRenameDevices`;
`NetReporting.renamesNetDevices` (`net` + `net_rename` + `net_rename_devices`); `Net`
`renamesDevices` (a connected Mac of the net takes it) and `rename(_ member: NetMember, to:)`,
through any such Mac; `NetSettings` offers Rename (menu and swipe) on each row of Portals,
this device's included, only while a connected Mac takes it, with an alert like Rename
thread's. A rename that cannot go says why ("Not changed").
